Reflexion improves on the basic ReAct approach by incorporating a process of self-reflection and memory utilization. While ReAct augments the agent's action space to include reasoning traces that help in decomposing tasks and updating context, Reflexion goes further by enabling the agent to learn from its past experiences through self-reflection. This involves the agent recognizing when a different action could have led to better outcomes and storing this experience in memory. Reflexion uses both short-term and long-term memory to condition its decisions, allowing for rapid improvement in decision-making by leveraging informative feedback signals. This iterative process of trial, error, and self-reflection enhances the agent's ability to adapt and perform better in various environments.
